# Echelon Diagnostic Framework (EDF)

## Purpose

The Echelon Diagnostic Framework (EDF) is a structured approach for understanding systems, diagnosing problems, explaining successes, and identifying the highest-leverage points for intervention.

EDF is designed to answer:

> What is happening?

Before asking:

> What should we do?

EDF complements decision frameworks such as Clarity by establishing confidence in the current state of reality before decisions are evaluated.

---

# Core Philosophy

Many investigations stop after identifying a root cause.

EDF recognizes that:

- Symptoms often appear far from their true origins.
- Multiple causes frequently contribute to outcomes.
- The best intervention point is often different from where the problem originated.
- Successes can be analyzed with the same rigor as failures.

The goal is not merely to find fault.

The goal is to understand systems.

---

# Guiding Principles

## 1. Diagnose Before Prescribing

Do not discuss solutions until sufficient understanding has been achieved.

Bad outcomes often result from:

```text
Observation
↓
Assumption
↓
Solution
```

EDF seeks:

```text
Observation
↓
Evidence
↓
Model
↓
Hypothesis
↓
Testing
↓
Understanding
↓
Action
```

---

## 2. Reality Before Decisions

EDF focuses on understanding reality.

Decision frameworks focus on selecting actions.

Understanding and decision-making are separate activities.

---

## 3. Control Matters More Than Blame

EDF seeks:

- What happened
- Why it happened
- Where intervention has leverage

The framework prioritizes future prevention over historical fault assignment.

---

## 4. Successes Are Diagnosed Like Failures

EDF can be used to explain:

- Failures
- Incidents
- Operational problems
- Successful outcomes
- Repeatable excellence

Understanding why something worked is often as valuable as understanding why it failed.

---

# Core Concepts

## Manifestation

The observable outcome.

Examples:

- Slippery pool deck
- Software outage
- Missed deadlines
- Successful moon landing
- Revenue decline

Question:

> What do we observe?

---

## Origin

The location where contributing causes began.

Origins may be:

### Physical Origins

Examples:

- Failed O-ring
- Burned-out capacitor
- Incorrect sealer

### System Origins

Examples:

- Weak governance
- Poor requirements gathering
- Risk normalization
- Inadequate review processes

Question:

> Where did this begin?

---

## Control Layer

The point where intervention can most effectively influence future outcomes.

The control layer may differ from the origin layer.

Question:

> Where can we most effectively intervene?

Examples:

| Origin | Control |
|----------|----------|
| Wrong sealer | Material approval process |
| Developer error | Code review process |
| O-ring failure | Engineering authority |
| Scope churn | Product governance |

---

## Propagation

The path through which effects travel.

Example:

```text
Requirements Failure
↓
Design Decision
↓
Material Selection
↓
Installation
↓
Slippery Deck
```

Question:

> How did the outcome emerge?

---

## Origin Network

Complex outcomes often have multiple contributing origins.

Instead of:

```text
One Root Cause
```

EDF allows:

```text
Outcome
├── Origin A
├── Origin B
└── Origin C
```

This reflects the reality of most complex systems.

---

# Evidence Hierarchy

Not all evidence carries equal weight.

Example hierarchy:

| Level | Evidence Type |
|---------|---------|
| 1 | Opinion |
| 2 | Experience |
| 3 | Observation |
| 4 | Measurement |
| 5 | Documentation |
| 6 | Independent Verification |
| 7 | Physical Proof |

Higher confidence should be placed in stronger evidence.

---

# Complexity Levels

EDF scales based on problem complexity.

---

## EDF-0 (Rapid)

Used for simple problems.

### Output

- Manifestation
- Origin
- Control
- Confidence

### Example

```text
Manifestation:
No light

Origin:
Burned-out bulb

Control:
Replace bulb

Confidence:
High
```

---

## EDF-1 (Standard)

Used for diagnosable problems with multiple possible causes.

### Output

- Manifestation
- Origin
- Control
- Confidence
- Evidence
- Alternative Explanations

### Example

```text
Manifestation:
House not cooling

Possible Origins:
Capacitor
Compressor
Thermostat

Control:
Further testing

Confidence:
Medium
```

---

## EDF-2 (Complex)

Used when:

- Multiple teams are involved
- Multiple systems interact
- Human decisions matter
- Long feedback loops exist
- Causes are disputed

### Output

- Manifestation
- Propagation
- Origin Network
- Control Layers
- Evidence
- Competing Hypotheses
- Confidence
- Unknowns
- Recommendations

---

# Escalation Triggers

EDF expands only when necessary.

---

## Trigger 1

Origin uncertain.

Action:

Generate competing hypotheses.

---

## Trigger 2

Multiple causes possible.

Action:

Build propagation model.

---

## Trigger 3

Control differs from origin.

Action:

Perform echelon analysis.

---

## Trigger 4

Multiple stakeholders involved.

Action:

Use evidence hierarchy.

---

# Diagnostic Sufficiency

A key EDF principle.

Question:

> Do we know enough to act responsibly?

Not:

> Do we know everything?

Diagnosis should stop when understanding is sufficient for action.

Avoid analysis paralysis.

---

# Echelons

Problems often manifest at different layers than where they originate.

Examples:

| Manifestation Layer | Origin Layer |
|---------------------|--------------|
| Slippery deck | Requirements failure |
| Production outage | Architecture decision |
| Missed deadlines | Governance issue |
| Revenue decline | Customer retention problem |

The larger the system, the more likely causes and symptoms exist in different echelons.

# Relationship to Clarity

EDF and Clarity serve different purposes.

---

## EDF

Primary Question:

> What is happening?

Outputs:

- Manifestation
- Origins
- Propagation
- Control Points
- Evidence
- Confidence

---

## Clarity

Primary Question:

> What should we do?

Outputs:

- Options
- Assumptions
- Risks
- Ownership
- Reevaluation Triggers
- Decisions

---

```text
Observation

EDF

↓
```

## 1. Manifestation

What happened?

---

## 2. Origin

---

## 3. Propagation

How did it spread through the system?

---

## 4. Control

Where can intervention create the greatest leverage?

---

# Final Objective

The objective of EDF is not merely to identify root causes.

The objective is to understand systems well enough to:

- Explain outcomes
- Increase confidence
- Identify leverage points
- Improve future decisions
- Create repeatable success
- Prevent recurring failures

Understanding precedes action.

EDF exists to create that understanding.
