# EDF — Customer Demand

## Evidence
- **Demand for diagnosis exists mainly where it is mandated**: Joint Commission sentinel-event analysis (45 business days), FDA QMSR/ISO 13485 CAPA (effective 2026-02-02), ISO 9001 10.2, OSHA PSM incident investigation, Part 135 SMS (2027) (SRC-H-023..028). Most mandated settings exclude a small uncredentialed firm through credentials, liability and procurement; ISO 9001 SMEs are the accessible exception.
- **In unmandated settings, nobody in the sample asks for a "diagnostic grammar"** (CLM-C-017). EDF-relevant complaints cluster in RCA and incident learning (~38% of 96 VoC items map to EDF or both), where free norms exist (blameless postmortems, Cook's "How Complex Systems Fail", CAST/STAMP).
- **The documented failure is after diagnosis**: healthcare RCAs produce mostly weak actions — training 20%, process change 19.6%, policy reinforcement 15.2% — and events recur (Kellogg 2017); RCA is criticised for single-cause reduction and ritualisation (Peerally 2017); NERC root-cause identification rate ~55% (SRC-H-056).
- **AI substitutes for the written diagnosis**: EX-EDF-001 found a plain prompt at least as good as EDF on synthetic cases (REPO: research/registries/evidence-registry.json).

## Implication
Buyers pay to stop recurrence and to satisfy auditors, not for a better-structured diagnosis document. EDF's commercial relevance depends on whether its constructs (explicit alternatives, origin network, control points) lead to **stronger actions that are verified**, which has never been tested (HY-EDF-004 unsupported).


## Second-round buyer evidence (v3, 2026-11-16)

39 first-person items from engineering leaders (`customer-research/buyer-voice.csv` in the combined repo; ~16 are vendor testimonials, 0 owners or PE operating partners):
- The follow-through problem is real in leaders' words ("assigning a follow-up action was no guarantee that it would be completed"; "everything after the initial alert is where it fell apart").
- Every documented case was solved with tooling plus internal ownership; none hired an outside reviewer; one mandate was "without spending more".
- Respected practitioners de-emphasise action-item tracking (Honeycomb, Allspaw).
- Implication: demand for an outsider is unproven; the strongest-evidenced demand is for **internal capability** — which favours enablement (workshop + templates) over outsourced review if paid outsider demand fails to appear.
