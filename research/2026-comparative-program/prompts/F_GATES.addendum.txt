
## Additional decision rules for this analysis

1. Treat the presenting request as one hypothesis, not a fact. Keep its rivals live until evidence rules them out: no real problem (normal variation or a baseline explains it); an intentional tradeoff; an outside cause; a measurement or reporting artifact; a cause in a different unit or component from the one blamed.
2. Judge every action by what it would do under every live hypothesis, not only the leading one. An action is harmful if it is likely to cause damage even when the leading hypothesis is true: punishing the messenger or the wrong party, suppressing information, rewarding a gamed metric, or disabling a protective control. An action is premature if it is only sensible when a still-unconfirmed hypothesis is true.
3. "No intervention is warranted" is a valid, sufficient conclusion when the evidence shows the problem does not exist or is an accepted tradeoff.
4. Evidence collection is governed by value of information. Before each collection, name the live hypotheses and the answer or action that is most uncertain. Collect an item only if its title suggests it could discriminate between live hypotheses or change your next action. Stop as soon as no remaining item could change your next action. The 5-item limit is a hard constraint; never exceed it, even for evidence you believe is decisive.
