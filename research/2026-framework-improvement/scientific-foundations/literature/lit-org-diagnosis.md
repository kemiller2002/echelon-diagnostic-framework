# Literature Review: Organizational Diagnosis as a Field (Workstreams B + H)

Status: staging draft for synthesis. Retrieved 2026-11-16.
Companion file: `competing-methodologies.md`. Sources: `sources-BH.csv` (prefix `SRC-BH-`).

Verification conventions:

- **fetched**: page or PDF read.
- **search-result**: only abstracts or summaries were seen.
- **[GK]**: general knowledge; not used as evidence.

Each claim below is tagged:

- **E (empirical)**: data-based.
- **T (theoretical or proposed)**: argued, not tested.
- **P (practitioner consensus)**: widely practiced, untested.

## 1. What "organizational diagnosis" is

Organizational diagnosis is the use of conceptual models and applied research methods to assess an organization's current state and to find ways to solve problems, meet challenges, or improve performance [GK; consistent with the publisher descriptions of Harrison (SRC-BH-056) and Cummings and Worley's definition of diagnosis as understanding how the organization is currently functioning in order to design change interventions (SRC-BH-061)].

Three lineages are relevant to EDF.

### 1.1 The clinical lineage (Levinson; Alderfer)

- Levinson's *Organizational Diagnosis* (1972) sets out a long case-study procedure (SRC-BH-057). It includes:
  - "genetic" (historical) data;
  - interpretive data on functioning, attitudes, and relationships;
  - a feedback stage.
- Later commentators describe Levinson's method as psychoanalytically informed "immersion" diagnosis, linked to Lewin's action research (SRC-BH-057 search context). **(T/P)**
- Alderfer's "The Methodology of Organizational Diagnosis" (1980, *Professional Psychology* 11:459–468) belongs to this lineage and treats diagnosis as a relationship between the diagnostician and the client system (SRC-BH-058). Only the citation was located; the content was not retrieved, so its specific claims are not used here.

Relevance to EDF: the clinical lineage insists on **history**, meaning how the system got here. EDF's Origin Network can include historical origins but does not prompt for a timeline.

### 1.2 The model-driven OD lineage (Weisbord; Nadler and Tushman; Burke and Litwin; Cummings and Worley)

- From the mid-1970s, OD diagnosis was organized around **content models** that specify which components to examine and how they should fit (SRC-BH-006, SRC-BH-008, SRC-BH-002, SRC-BH-061).
- Falletta's review of diagnostic models describes these models chronologically. It credits Burke with the view that models help organize and interpret data and give a shared vocabulary. It notes that causal modeling (path analysis, structural equation modeling) is the way to test such models, and observes that the models emphasize internal variables (SRC-BH-060; unofficial copy, search-result only). **(T)**

Relevance: these models supply **coverage**, which EDF lacks for organizations. Their validation record is weak (section 3).

### 1.3 The applied-research and evidence-based lineage (Harrison; Harrison and Shirom; McFillen et al.)

- Harrison's *Diagnosing Organizations* (1987; 3rd ed. 2005) treats diagnosis as three linked concerns (SRC-BH-056):
  - **models** for framing problems and identifying underlying conditions;
  - **methods** for gathering and analysing data;
  - **processes** for working with clients.
- Harrison and Shirom (1999) introduce **"sharp-image diagnosis"**. Among its themes are using **multiple theoretical frames** and how to choose and combine them (SRC-BH-055, publisher table of contents). **(T)**
- McFillen, O'Neil, Balzer and Varney (2013) make the following points (SRC-BH-059):
  - Diagnosis is widely called integral to change, yet "the diagnostic process itself has received little study".
  - **Lack of rigour and the resulting misdiagnoses are likely significant factors in the high failure rate of change initiatives.** This is a hypothesis, not a measured finding.
  - They propose an evidence-based diagnostic approach modelled on **medical diagnosis**, chosen over engineering diagnosis because engineering does not account for human behaviour.
  - Status: **T**.

### 1.4 The dialogic critique (Bushe and Marshak)

- Bushe and Marshak (2009) distinguish "**diagnostic OD**", which assumes an objective reality that can be assessed and then changed, from "**dialogic OD**", which assumes organizations are socially constructed and that changing conversations and narratives is the change mechanism (SRC-BH-062; content as summarized in search results). **(T)**
- A related philosophical objection holds that organizational "truth" is socially constructed, which challenges empirical verification of diagnoses (search-result context; Hatley 2017 review, not separately catalogued).

Relevance to EDF: EDF is unambiguously a **diagnostic-OD** method; it assumes a reality to be established ("establishes confidence in reality"). The dialogic critique carries two practical implications.

- (a) **Diagnosis is itself an intervention.** Interviews and surveys change the system being diagnosed [GK; a standard OD tenet consistent with SRC-BH-062].
- (b) In some organizational problems, the "outcome" and "manifestations" are contested meanings, not facts. EDF already requires factual outcome statements (spec §2). That is defensible, but EDF should acknowledge contested framings in System Context rather than silently resolve them.

## 2. Adjacent diagnostic sciences with stronger evidence

Organizational diagnosis has little empirical study of its own process. The most useful evidence for EDF comes from adjacent fields that *have* studied diagnostic reasoning.

### 2.1 Medical diagnostic error (Workstream H)

- Graber, Franklin and Gordon (2005) studied 100 diagnostic-error cases in internal medicine. **E** (SRC-BH-070, fetched)
  - 90 cases involved injury, including 33 deaths.
  - 548 contributing factors were identified across 93 cases, an average of **5.9 per case**.
  - System-related factors contributed to 65% of cases and cognitive factors to 74%.
  - **Premature closure** (failing to keep considering reasonable alternatives once an initial diagnosis is reached) was the single most common cognitive cause.
  - Knowledge deficits were uncommon.
  - Their conclusion: diagnostic error is "commonly multifactorial in origin".
- A narrative review of cognitive interventions found 141 articles. Only 42 reported tested interventions, and most proposed debiasing interventions had not been formally tested (SRC-BH-071, search-result). **E (that evidence is lacking)**

Implications for EDF:

- Multifactorial causation is the empirical norm, which supports EDF's origin network.
- The leading failure mode of diagnosticians is premature closure. **EDF's Diagnostic Sufficiency step is exactly where premature closure would occur.** EDF currently asks whether more investigation would change the next action, but does not require that **live alternatives** be recorded and shown not to change that action.

### 2.2 Structured analytic techniques (intelligence analysis)

- Dhami, Belton and Mandel (2019) randomly assigned 50 intelligence analysts to use the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) or not. **E** (SRC-BH-072, fetched)
  - ACH users did not follow all steps.
  - Evidence that ACH reduces confirmation bias was mixed.
  - ACH **may increase judgment inconsistency and error**.
  - A later summary reports that ACH users made less use of base rates (search-result).

Implication: adding a heavyweight hypothesis matrix to EDF is **not** evidence-based. Lighter contrast-based discrimination (section 2.3) has better indirect support. Any structured step EDF adds should be tested, not assumed beneficial.

### 2.3 Troubleshooting and structured diagnostic strategy (human factors)

- Schaafstal, Schraagen and van Berlo (2000) developed "structured troubleshooting" from a cognitive task analysis. It combines a **domain-independent diagnostic strategy** with **context-specific, multi-level functional models** of the system. Technicians trained this way solved about **twice as many malfunctions, in less time**, than those given traditional training. **E** (SRC-BH-048, fetched)
- De Mast and Lokkerbol (2012) compared Six Sigma DMAIC with problem-solving theory. They found its most prominent weakness to be poor support for **efficient diagnosis**: cause-finding techniques are scattered and offer little strategic guidance for search. They attribute part of the problem to generality, since generic methods cannot use task-specific knowledge. **T (analytic, literature-based)** (SRC-BH-019, fetched)

Implication: the best-supported recipe is a **generic strategy plus a domain model**. EDF provides only the generic strategy. For organizational use, EDF likely needs an organizational "functional model" (content prompts drawn from Burke-Litwin, Nadler-Tushman or Weisbord) to get the benefit Schaafstal et al. observed. This is an inference by analogy from equipment troubleshooting to organizations, not a demonstrated transfer.

### 2.4 Prospective hindsight

- Klein's premortem asks a team to imagine that the plan has already failed and explain why (SRC-BH-073, search-result).
- It draws on Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989) on temporal perspective in explaining events (SRC-BH-074, search-result).
- Popular sources claim about a 30% gain in the number of reasons generated. Secondary commentary notes the gain was in *quantity* of reasons, not *quality*. This was not verified against the 1989 paper.
- Status: **E (weakly verified here)**.

Implication for EDF: a cheap "pre-mortem on the next responsible action" could sit in the Sufficiency step. Treat it as plausible, not proven.

## 3. Validation status of organizational diagnostic models

### 3.1 Summary judgment

Across the sources located, **no organizational diagnostic model has published evidence of diagnostic accuracy**: inter-diagnostician reliability, criterion validity against a reference diagnosis, or evidence that model-guided diagnosis leads to better intervention choices than unguided diagnosis.

The evidence that does exist falls into five types:

| Evidence type | Examples | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Exploratory utility studies | Burke-Litwin in one multinational (SRC-BH-003, fetched) | Weak; no psychometrics |
| Partial hypothesis tests | Burke-Litwin dissertation, 4 of 15 hypotheses supported (SRC-BH-004, search-result, unverified numbers) | Weak and mixed |
| Face/content validity of locally built questionnaires | Weisbord applications (SRC-BH-007) | Very weak |
| Construct–performance meta-analysis | Competing Values Framework: culture types relate to effectiveness, mixed nomological validity, internal structure partly unsupported (SRC-BH-044, fetched); network position predicts job performance (SRC-BH-037) | Moderate for constructs; says nothing about diagnostic accuracy |
| Instrument psychometrics reviews | "Reliability and validity of most [culture effectiveness] instruments is quite limited"; an earlier review found no evidence for 10 of 18 culture surveys; a review of 70 culture instruments found 60% of evaluative judgements had no statistical analyses available (SRC-BH-043, fetched) | Documents absence of validation |

One caveat on SRC-BH-043: two of its three authors are affiliated with Denison Consulting, a commercial vendor of a culture survey. Their critique of *other* instruments should be read with that conflict of interest in mind. The general conclusion matches the independent reviews they cite.

**Older OD evaluation literature:** a 1978 review in *Group & Organization Studies* found that only 3 of 26 OD studies met all validity criteria (search-result summary only; not catalogued). This is historical context consistent with the current picture.

### 3.2 The intervention-outcome literature is also weak, which compounds the problem

Even if a diagnosis is right, the downstream interventions that diagnostic methods recommend have weak or mixed outcome evidence:

- **Lean in healthcare:** no significant association with patient satisfaction or health outcomes, and inconsistent process benefits (SRC-BH-017, fetched).
- **Appreciative Inquiry in healthcare:** minimal empirical evidence; 1 RCT among 33 studies (SRC-BH-053, fetched). No evidence it outperforms other OD tools (SRC-BH-054).
- **Balanced Scorecard:** mixed (SRC-BH-014, SRC-BH-015).
- **Enterprise Architecture:** only about half of claims backed by empirical evidence, and outcomes are attributed without considering alternatives (SRC-BH-038, fetched).
- **Theory of Constraints:** 80+ successes and no failures reported, which suggests publication bias (SRC-BH-020, SRC-BH-021).
- **Root Cause Analysis:** identifies causes, but evidence that it prevents recurrence is weak (SRC-BH-026, SRC-BH-027, SRC-BH-075).

Methodological lesson: validation designs in this field typically lack controls, rely on self-selected success cases, and attribute outcomes without considering alternatives. **EDF's own validation programme should be designed specifically to avoid these three failure patterns**: pre-register, include failures and null results, and compare against a baseline method.

### 3.3 Where diagnostic-method reliability *has* been measured: accident analysis

Systemic accident analysis is the closest methodological sibling to EDF:

- multi-level origin networks;
- propagation through a sociotechnical system;
- control structures.

Its findings are the best available prior for EDF's likely reliability. **E** throughout.

- **Salmon, Cornelissen and Trotter (2012).** Accimap, HFACS and STAMP were each applied by a separate analyst to the same incident. The outputs differed significantly. The authors recommend Accimap combined with domain-specific failure taxonomies (SRC-BH-065, search-result).
- **Jun, Waterson and Filho (2018).** Four analyses of one accident, two with each method. Reliability, measured as causal-factor overlap between analysts using the same method, was 65% for STAMP and 38% for Accimap. Validity, measured as overlap across methods, was 8% (SRC-BH-066, search-result).
- **Hulme, Stanton, Walker, Waterson and Salmon (2021).** Criterion-referenced concurrent validity was tested with signal detection theory against a referent expert analysis, with "analytical fragmentation" to attribute validity to each method phase. Results were "comparable and moderate" and judged not satisfactory (SRC-BH-067, fetched).
- **Later reliability study (2023, *Ergonomics*).** Weak-to-moderate inter-rater correlations; STAMP-CAST significantly higher than AcciMap and AcciNet (SRC-BH-068, search-result).
- **Taxonomy coding (HFACS).** Low category-level consensus in at least one real-world setting (SRC-BH-069, search-result).
- **FMEA.** Two hospital teams analysing the same process did not reproduce each other's results (SRC-BH-032, secondary). The outputs missed the most common real failure type, omitted doses, and the risk priority number is mathematically invalid because it multiplies ordinal scales (SRC-BH-031, fetched).

**Interpretation:**

1. EDF analyses of the same organizational case by different analysts should be **expected** to diverge substantially. That is the base rate in the most comparable literature.
2. More **constrained representations** (STAMP's controller, control-action and feedback structure) appear to raise reliability relative to free-form maps. This is suggestive, from a small number of studies.
3. Validity against a referent is moderate even for mature methods.
4. These studies provide a **ready-made, published measurement protocol** that EDF can adopt directly: causal-factor overlap, signal-detection hits and false alarms against a referent, and phase-level fragmentation.

### 3.4 Systems-dynamics evidence on why diagnoses fail to produce improvement

Repenning and Sterman (2001) argue, from field studies and system dynamics models, that improvement programmes fail not because of the techniques but because of how the techniques interact with the physical, economic, social and psychological structures they enter (SRC-BH-022, fetched). Their summary includes the capability trap: managers fail to connect problems to past decisions and attribute them to worker motivation, a self-confirming cycle (SRC-BH-022 search context). **E (case-based) / T (model)**

Implication: organizational diagnoses are biased by the attributions of the people interviewed, especially leaders. Feedback structure, not single causes, often produces persistent organizational outcomes.

### 3.5 Context-dependence of identical interventions

Bushe (2010) studied eight sites in one school district. All received the same Appreciative Inquiry process, training and facilitators. Four transformed, two changed incrementally, and two showed little or no change. Summit positive affect did not predict change. Generativity, the quality of the discovery phase, leader credibility and links to pre-existing shared concerns did (SRC-BH-052, fetched). **E (comparative case, small n)**

Implication: this supports EDF's Context Principle ("valid only within its declared System Context") and argues that **local contextual moderators** should be named as part of control-point confidence.

## 5. Opportunities for EDF

Ordered roughly by expected value given the evidence. Each entry gives:

- **Mechanism**: what to add or change, and where it sits in the 9-step grammar.
- **Evidence strength**: Strong, Moderate, Weak, or Theoretical, for the underlying mechanism (not for its transfer to EDF).
- **Applicability**: how directly the evidence transfers to organizational EDF use, and at which complexity level.

### O1. Adopt the accident-analysis reliability/validity protocol for EDF validation
- **Mechanism:** For benchmark cases, have at least two independent analysts produce EDF analyses.
  - Report **origin-set overlap** (Jaccard or percentage agreement, as in SRC-BH-066).
  - Report **control-point ranking agreement**.
  - Score each analysis against a referent analysis with **signal detection** (hits, misses, false alarms), with **phase fragmentation** to locate which EDF step loses validity (SRC-BH-067).
  - Compare against a baseline method (for example free-form RCA or 5 Whys) and pre-register.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong as a measurement method (published, peer-reviewed); the targets are empirical baselines (38–65% reliability).
- **Applicability:** Direct. It fits the repository's benchmark harness (`benchmarks/`, `diagnostic-validity/`). It is the only route to evidence-based claims about EDF.

### O2. Add a discriminating-evidence (contrast) sub-step
- **Mechanism:** Between Origin Network (4) and Evaluate Evidence (6):
  - specify **IS / IS NOT** for the outcome (where, when, who, extent);
  - list **distinctions and changes**;
  - require each candidate origin to explain both the IS and the IS NOT.
  - Include **positive-deviance comparators**: similar units where the outcome did not occur (KT, SRC-BH-046; AI logic, SRC-BH-052).
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate (indirect). Structured diagnostic strategy improved troubleshooting in a controlled evaluation (SRC-BH-048). Generic methods are analytically weak at efficient diagnosis (SRC-BH-019). KT itself lacks independent validation.
- **Applicability:** High for EDF-1 and EDF-2 when comparison units exist (sites, teams, periods). Lower for one-off, whole-organization outcomes.

### O3. Guard Diagnostic Sufficiency against premature closure
- **Mechanism:** Diagnostic Sufficiency must list the **top competing explanations still alive**. It must state why each would *not* change the next responsible action, or else make discriminating among them the next action.
  - Optionally, run a brief **pre-mortem** on the next responsible action.
- **Evidence strength:**
  - Strong that premature closure is the dominant cognitive error in medicine (SRC-BH-070).
  - Weak that debiasing interventions work (SRC-BH-071).
  - ACH-style heavy matrices may harm (SRC-BH-072).
  - So keep it **lightweight** and test it.
- **Applicability:** All levels. At EDF-0 a single "alternative considered" line suffices.

### O4. Never aggregate the four ranking criteria arithmetically; anchor them
- **Mechanism:** State in step 8 that Influence, Controllability, Cost and Confidence are **ordinal** and must not be multiplied or summed. Use dominance or Pareto ordering plus explicit trade-off reasoning.
  - Anchor **Influence/durability** with the **RCA2 Action Hierarchy**: structural or forcing-function changes rank stronger than procedural changes, which rank stronger than training or reminders (SRC-BH-028, SRC-BH-029).
- **Evidence strength:**
  - Strong (mathematical plus empirical) that ordinal-product scores mislead (SRC-BH-031).
  - Moderate, as a human-factors-grounded consensus, for the action hierarchy.
- **Applicability:** Direct, all levels. It formalizes the spec's existing "no false precision" clause.

### O5. Make origin-network logic explicit (AND/OR) and propagation dynamic (loops/delays)
- **Mechanism:**
  - Mark origin links as **jointly necessary (AND)** or **independently sufficient (OR)**, borrowing fault tree gate semantics (SRC-BH-030).
  - Allow Propagation to include **reinforcing and balancing loops, delays and stocks**.
  - Flag control points with expected **worse-before-better** or side effects (SRC-BH-022, SRC-BH-023).
- **Evidence strength:**
  - Moderate. FTA logic is mathematically sound.
  - The capability-trap dynamics are field-supported.
  - The benefit of adding this notation to EDF is untested.
- **Applicability:** EDF-2 primarily. At EDF-1, an optional "is there a feedback loop?" prompt.

### O6. Provide an optional organizational content model and control-lever taxonomy
- **Mechanism:**
  - For organizational Primary Systems, offer **origin prompts** drawn from established models (strategy, structure, systems/processes, rewards, people/skills, leadership, culture/climate, formal-vs-informal gaps, external environment).
  - Offer a **control-lever taxonomy**, for example the Galbraith Star levers.
  - Keep the prompts as coverage aids, not as causal claims.
- **Evidence strength:**
  - Moderate by analogy: a domain model plus a generic strategy improves troubleshooting (SRC-BH-048), and taxonomies were recommended to improve systemic analysis (SRC-BH-065).
  - Weak for the specific organizational taxonomies, which are unvalidated (SRC-BH-003, SRC-BH-007).
  - Taxonomy coding can itself be unreliable (SRC-BH-069).
- **Applicability:** High for organizational use. Test whether the prompts raise inter-analyst agreement (via O1) before making them normative.

### O7. Consider a control-structure framing for Control Points
- **Mechanism:** Express each control point as **controller → control action → controlled process → feedback channel** (STAMP-CAST). Ask why existing controls failed: missing, inadequate, or delayed feedback, or a flawed process model (SRC-BH-064).
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate-suggestive. STAMP and CAST showed higher inter-analyst reliability than Accimap in two studies (SRC-BH-066, SRC-BH-068), though validity was still only moderate (SRC-BH-067).
- **Applicability:** EDF-2, and organizational governance problems. It aligns with EDF's "control points may differ from origins".

### O8. Define evidence tiers and quality criteria in Evaluate Evidence
- **Mechanism:** Tier the evidence, for example:
  - (a) system event data or measured records;
  - (b) direct observation;
  - (c) multiple independent testimonies;
  - (d) single testimony or leader attribution;
  - (e) inference.

  Apply the event-data criteria of **trustworthiness, completeness and defined semantics**, and **question-driven extraction** (SRC-BH-034). For survey-based culture or climate evidence, require attention to **aggregation** and **criterion validity** (SRC-BH-043). Treat leaders' causal attributions as hypotheses (SRC-BH-022).
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate. Expert consensus (process mining), psychometric reviews (culture), and field evidence of attribution bias.
- **Applicability:** All levels. This step is currently underspecified in EDF ("separate direct observation from inference").

### O9. Make "probe as next responsible action" explicit for complex situations
- **Mechanism:** If the situation is judged complex (cause and effect only coherent in retrospect), Diagnostic Sufficiency may be reached by designing a **safe-to-fail probe** that produces discriminating evidence, rather than by more analysis (SRC-BH-049, SRC-BH-050).
- **Evidence strength:** Theoretical (Cynefin is unvalidated). It is consistent with the context-dependence evidence (SRC-BH-052) and with the moderate validity ceiling of retrospective analysis (SRC-BH-067).
- **Applicability:** EDF-1 and EDF-2 organizational analyses with high uncertainty.

### O10. Add sustainment and monitoring after the next responsible action
- **Mechanism:** For each top control point, name a **leading indicator** and a review date. This borrows from DMAIC Control and Balanced Scorecard practice, and gives the Validate verb data (SRC-BH-012, SRC-BH-019).
  - Treat hypothesized propagation chains as hypotheses until indicators confirm them (Nørreklit's critique, SRC-BH-013).
- **Evidence strength:** Weak to moderate. BSC outcome evidence is mixed (SRC-BH-014, SRC-BH-015). RCA's weak recurrence-prevention record (SRC-BH-026) suggests follow-through is a common gap.
- **Applicability:** All levels. At EDF-0 a single "how will we know" line suffices.

### O11. Record diagnostic reactivity and stakeholder framing in System Context
- **Mechanism:** Note who was interviewed or surveyed, how the inquiry may change the system, and where stakeholders frame the outcome differently (the dialogic critique, SRC-BH-062; multiframe diagnosis, SRC-BH-055). Keep the outcome statement factual; record contested framings separately.
- **Evidence strength:** Theoretical.
- **Applicability:** Organizational EDF-1 and EDF-2. Low cost.

### O12. Treat Diagnostic Calibration lenses as "multiframe diagnosis", and test them
- **Mechanism:** Harrison and Shirom's multiframe diagnosis (SRC-BH-055) is the established theoretical precedent for EDF's Analytical Lenses. Use O1 metrics to test whether lenses add distinct, valid origins. Harrison and Shirom provide theory, not evidence that multiframe diagnosis improves accuracy.
- **Evidence strength:** Theoretical. The precedent strengthens conceptual grounding, not empirical standing.
- **Applicability:** The Diagnostic Calibration extension.

## 6. Honest gaps in this review

- Several key primary texts were **not** read in full: Harrison 2005, Harrison and Shirom 1999, Levinson 1972, Alderfer 1980, Cummings and Worley, Burke and Litwin 1992, Weisbord 1976 (the PDF was a non-text scan), Peerally et al. 2017, and Jun et al. 2018 (HTTP 403). Claims about them rest on publisher descriptions, abstracts or search summaries, and are marked search-result in the CSV.
- No search located any study of **inter-consultant agreement in organizational (non-accident) diagnosis**. Its absence from these searches is itself a finding, but a systematic database search (PsycINFO, Business Source) would be needed to confirm it.
- Effect sizes from Fang et al. (2015), Shah and Ward (2003) and the Burke-Litwin dissertation were seen only in summaries and should be checked before quantitative use.
- The prospective-hindsight "30%" figure was not verified against Mitchell et al. (1989).
