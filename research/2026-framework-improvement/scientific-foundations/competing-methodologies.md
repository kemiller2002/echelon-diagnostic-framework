# Competing Diagnostic Methodologies: Comparative Inventory (Workstreams B + H)

Status: staging draft for synthesis. Retrieved 2026-11-16.
Scope: established methods an organization could use instead of, or alongside, EDF v0.3 (SRC-BH-001).

## How to read this document

- Source IDs (`SRC-BH-nnn`) refer to `sources-BH.csv`. Each source there has a verification level: **fetched** (the page or PDF was read), or **search-result** (only abstracts or summaries in search results were seen). No source is cited from memory.
- Method descriptions marked **[GK]** come from general professional knowledge of a widely documented method (for example, the names of the 7S elements) and were not checked against a primary text in this session. They describe the methods; they are not evidence claims.
- **"Validation"** has a narrow meaning here: has anyone shown, empirically, that the method *diagnoses correctly*? That means inter-analyst reliability, agreement with a reference diagnosis, or prediction of which intervention works. That is a separate question from (a) whether the method's underlying constructs correlate with performance, (b) whether organizations that adopted it improved, and (c) whether practitioners like it. Most entries only have (b) or (c).

### Top-line finding

No organizational diagnostic model reviewed here has published evidence of its **diagnostic accuracy**, meaning that two teams using it on the same organization reach the same diagnosis and that the diagnosis is correct.

The closest analogues come from accident analysis. There, inter-analyst reliability of systemic methods is low to moderate: 38% overlap for Accimap and 65% for STAMP, with only 8% cross-method agreement (SRC-BH-066). Validity against an expert referent is "moderate" and judged unsatisfactory (SRC-BH-067).

EDF should therefore not assume that competitors are validated. It should equally not assume that its own grammar is reliable. The repository says as much about Diagnostic Calibration (docs/edf/diagnostic-calibration.md, "Evidence Boundary").

## A. Organizational content models (what to look at)

These models tell the diagnostician which organizational components to examine and assume that performance depends on how well those components fit together. EDF is a **process grammar** with no content model. That is the main structural difference.

### A1. McKinsey 7S

- **Intended problem:** Misalignment among organizational elements during strategy change or reorganization.
- **Mechanism:** Assess seven interdependent elements and check their consistency: Strategy, Structure, Systems, Shared values, Style, Staff, Skills [GK; origin in Waterman, Peters and Phillips 1980, *Business Horizons*, per SRC-BH-010]. Peters describes the "hard" and "soft" S's as having no precedence over each other (SRC-BH-010).
- **Required evidence:** Interviews, documents, management judgment. There is no standard instrument.
- **Strengths:** Simple, memorable, and makes "soft" factors legitimate objects of diagnosis.
- **Limitations:**
  - No causal ordering, so it cannot say which misfit produced an outcome.
  - Static.
  - It does not separate evidence from inference.
- **Scientific validation:** None located. The model's best-known application, *In Search of Excellence*, has been criticized for its sample selection (SRC-BH-010 context). Status: practitioner adoption only.
- **Cost/time:** Low to moderate (workshops and interviews).
- **Reproducibility:** Low. Element ratings depend on who is judging.
- **Adoption:** Very high in consulting and teaching.
- **What EDF could learn:** Use the seven elements as a prompt list of **organizational origin categories**. EDF's origin types (technical, human, organizational, ...) leave "organizational" undifferentiated.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Coverage of soft organizational factors, and immediate familiarity to executives.

### A2. Burke-Litwin Causal Model of Organizational Performance and Change

- **Intended problem:** Explaining organizational performance and planning change. It separates transformational factors (environment, mission/strategy, leadership, culture) from transactional factors (structure, systems, management practices, climate, motivation, and others) (SRC-BH-002; factor listing [GK]).
- **Mechanism:** A hypothesized causal ordering. External environment drives transformational factors, which drive transactional factors, which drive performance. It includes feedback loops [GK].
- **Required evidence:** Typically organization-wide surveys, such as a Burke-Litwin organizational assessment survey (SRC-BH-005), plus interviews.
- **Strengths:** It is the only major organizational development (OD) model that claims explicit causal direction, which makes it testable in principle. It also separates fast-moving from slow-moving factors.
- **Limitations:** The causal arrows are theoretical. The model is heavy to apply and survey-dependent.
- **Scientific validation:** Weak.
  - The most cited "test", Martins and Coetzee (2009), is an exploratory single-company study using focus groups (N=147) and interviews (N=11). It assessed the model's *utility* as a framework, with no psychometric or causal test (SRC-BH-003, fetched).
  - A Michigan State dissertation reportedly found 4 of 15 hypotheses supported and 8 partially supported (SRC-BH-004; search summary only, numbers not checked).
  - No large-sample confirmatory test of the full causal structure was located.
  - Status: partial construct-level testing; no diagnostic-accuracy evidence.
- **Cost/time:** High (survey design and administration, analysis over weeks to months).
- **Reproducibility:** Moderate if a standard survey is used; low for interview-based use.
- **Adoption:** High in OD consulting and academic teaching.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - Distinguish **slow, deep origins** (strategy, culture, leadership) from **fast, operational origins**. That split is useful for control-point ranking because deep origins have high influence but low short-term controllability.
  - Burke-Litwin's explicit causal direction is a reminder that EDF's "Propagation" should state direction and time lag.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** It offers a testable causal content model and a standard survey route to organization-wide evidence.

### A3. Nadler-Tushman Congruence Model

- **Intended problem:** Diagnosing performance gaps as misfit among the work, people, formal organization, and informal organization/culture, given the inputs and strategy (SRC-BH-008).
- **Mechanism:** Inputs (environment, resources, history) feed strategy, which feeds a transformation process with four components, which produces outputs. The diagnostician identifies output gaps, then assesses pairwise fit among the components [GK].
- **Required evidence:** Performance data on outputs, interviews, observation.
- **Strengths:** Starts from **output gaps**, which is close to EDF's Outcome-first logic. Pairwise-fit analysis is systematic.
- **Limitations:**
  - "Fit" is hard to operationalize.
  - Equilibrium-oriented.
  - No explicit evidence-grading step.
  - Martins and Coetzee considered it "too complex" for their study's aims (SRC-BH-003, fetched).
- **Scientific validation:** Applied empirically in at least one *Academy of Management Journal* study of environmental performance in electronics firms (SRC-BH-009; search-result only). The broader contingency-fit literature supports fit-performance relationships in general, but not this model's diagnostic accuracy. Status: some construct use; no diagnostic-accuracy evidence.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate.
- **Reproducibility:** Low to moderate.
- **Adoption:** High in executive education and consulting.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - A **gap-first** definition: compare the outcome with an expected or required outcome.
  - A **pairwise-fit check** as one way to generate candidate origins in organizational contexts.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Its theory of organizational components gives inexperienced diagnosticians more guidance than EDF does.

### A4. Weisbord Six-Box Model

- **Intended problem:** A quick, broad organizational checkup "with or without a theory" (SRC-BH-006).
- **Mechanism:** Six boxes: purposes, structure, relationships, rewards, leadership, helpful mechanisms. Each is examined for the gap between the formal system (what is supposed to happen) and the informal system (what actually happens). Interdependence among the boxes means single-factor fixes rarely work (SRC-BH-006). It led to the Organizational Diagnosis Questionnaire (SRC-BH-006 search summary).
- **Required evidence:** Interviews, questionnaires, observation.
- **Strengths:**
  - Very accessible.
  - The **formal-versus-informal gap** is a strong diagnostic heuristic.
  - It explicitly warns against single-factor fixes.
- **Limitations:** Internally focused, not causal, and box ratings are subjective.
- **Scientific validation:** Weisbord framed it as a practice theory. Applied studies typically report only face and content validity of locally built questionnaires (SRC-BH-007). No reliability or factor-analytic validation of the six-box structure was located. Status: practitioner adoption only.
- **Cost/time:** Low.
- **Reproducibility:** Low.
- **Adoption:** High in OD and healthcare management teaching.
- **What EDF could learn:** Add a **"formal vs actual" probe** to Manifestations and Origins. For example: what does the policy or org chart say happens, versus what the evidence shows happens?
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Speed, accessibility, and an explicit formal/informal lens.

### A5. Galbraith Star Model

- **Intended problem:** Organization *design* rather than diagnosis proper. It aligns strategy, structure, processes, rewards, and people (SRC-BH-011).
- **Mechanism:** Five design policies must be aligned. Galbraith argued that design efforts over-focus on the org chart (SRC-BH-011).
- **Required evidence:** Strategy documents, process and reward data.
- **Strengths:** Its components are directly controllable design levers, which map naturally onto EDF Control Points.
- **Limitations:** Assumes strategy is clear. Weak on external causes and on evidence.
- **Scientific validation:** None located for the model itself. Status: practitioner adoption.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate.
- **Reproducibility:** Low to moderate.
- **Adoption:** Moderate to high in organization-design practice.
- **What EDF could learn:** Use the Star's five levers as a **control-point taxonomy** for organizational analyses. EDF has an origin taxonomy but no control-point taxonomy.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** It links diagnosis directly to designable levers.

### A6. Cummings and Worley open-systems diagnosis (OD textbook standard)

- **Intended problem:** General OD diagnosis at organization, group, and job levels (SRC-BH-061).
- **Mechanism:** Inputs, transformations, and outputs, with feedback, boundaries, equifinality, and alignment among design components across three levels (SRC-BH-061, search summaries of earlier editions).
- **Evidence:** Mixed methods: questionnaires, interviews, observation, unobtrusive measures [GK].
- **Strengths:** Multi-level; equifinality explicitly allows different causal paths to the same outcome.
- **Limitations:** Generic and non-causal.
- **Validation:** None as a diagnostic instrument. It is a textbook framework.
- **What EDF could learn:** Make the **level of analysis** explicit (individual, group, organization, inter-organization) in System Context. Equifinality supports EDF's origin-network stance.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Multi-level structure.

## B. Performance-measurement and strategy-execution diagnostics

### B1. Balanced Scorecard (BSC)

- **Intended problem:** Translating strategy into a balanced set of measures across financial, customer, internal-process, and learning/growth perspectives (SRC-BH-012). Strategy maps add hypothesized cause-effect links [GK].
- **Mechanism:** Monitoring, not one-off diagnosis. Diagnosis comes from variance in the measures.
- **Evidence:** Ongoing KPI data.
- **Strengths:** Continuous evidence stream; links outcomes to leading indicators.
- **Limitations:** Nørreklit argues the BSC's causal claims are rhetorical and not grounded in proven theory (SRC-BH-013).
- **Validation:**
  - Outcome evidence is mixed. A survey of 76 business units found a positive association (SRC-BH-015).
  - A 2022 healthcare systematic review found only mild effects and high risk of bias (SRC-BH-014).
  - Status: mixed adoption-outcome evidence; the strategy-map causal logic is untested as a diagnostic.
- **Cost/time:** High to set up; low marginal cost afterwards.
- **Reproducibility:** High for measures, low for causal interpretation.
- **Adoption:** Very high.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - Pair a one-off EDF analysis with **leading indicators** for each top-ranked control point. That gives EDF's Validate verb an ongoing data stream.
  - Nørreklit's critique is a warning: hypothesized cause-effect chains such as Propagation must be marked as hypotheses until evidence exists.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Continuous monitoring and integration with management routines.

### B2. Strategy-execution diagnostics (Sull et al.; Rumelt)

- **Intended problem:** Why strategies fail in execution.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Sull, Homkes and Sull (2015) report a long-running survey of about 8,000 managers in 250 companies. They challenge "myths", for example that alignment equals execution or that communication equals understanding (SRC-BH-041; sample figures from search summary).
  - Rumelt's strategy "kernel" puts **diagnosis** first: an explanation of the nature of the challenge, followed by guiding policy and coherent action (SRC-BH-042, secondary).
- **Evidence:** Manager surveys and case analysis.
- **Strengths:** Large-sample descriptive data on coordination failures. Rumelt's framing ties diagnosis to action, as EDF's Diagnostic Sufficiency does.
- **Limitations:** Proprietary survey data; HBR articles are not peer reviewed.
- **Validation:** Descriptive survey only; no diagnostic-accuracy test.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - Rumelt's point that a good diagnosis simplifies the challenge enough to act on supports EDF's sufficiency criterion.
  - Sull et al. show that **perception gaps between levels** are themselves important manifestations. EDF could prompt for multi-level perspective sampling.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Benchmark data for comparison.

## C. Operations and quality improvement methods

### C1. Lean (including Value Stream Mapping)

- **Intended problem:** Waste and flow problems in operations.
- **Mechanism:** Map the value stream, quantify value-added versus non-value-added time, and find waste and flow interruptions [GK].
- **Evidence:** Direct observation (gemba), time studies, flow metrics.
- **Strengths:**
  - Direct observation of the work.
  - Quantified flow metrics.
  - Strong bias toward going to see.
- **Limitations:**
  - VSM is a static snapshot that ignores variability (search summaries; SRC-BH-033 context).
  - Weak on non-operational causes such as strategy, politics, and incentives.
- **Validation:**
  - Lean practice bundles explained about 23% of variance in plant operational performance after controls (SRC-BH-016; search summary).
  - In healthcare, a review of 22 quality-screened studies found no significant association with patient satisfaction or health outcomes, a negative association with worker satisfaction and costs, and inconsistent process benefits. "The evidence to date simply does not support this claim" (SRC-BH-017, fetched).
  - VSM effectiveness evidence in care settings is limited (SRC-BH-033).
  - Status: practice-outcome associations in manufacturing; weak elsewhere; no diagnostic-accuracy evidence.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate (VSM takes days); a Lean transformation takes years.
- **Reproducibility:** Moderate to high for measured flow metrics.
- **Adoption:** Very high.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - Require **direct observation** of manifestations where feasible, as an evidence tier above reports.
  - Add an option to quantify propagation along process flows: time, queue, rework.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Quantified, observable process evidence and well-developed tooling.

### C2. Six Sigma / DMAIC

- **Intended problem:** Reducing variation and defects in repeatable processes.
- **Mechanism:** Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control, with statistical verification of causes (hypothesis tests, designed experiments) [GK].
- **Evidence:** Process data; statistically adequate samples.
- **Strengths:**
  - Strong statistical fact-finding and empirical verification of ideas.
  - The staged model structures the problem (SRC-BH-019, fetched).
  - The **Control** phase builds in sustainment.
- **Limitations:**
  - De Mast and Lokkerbol identify "inferior methodology for efficient problem diagnosis" as DMAIC's most prominent weakness. Cause-finding techniques are scattered and give little strategic guidance for an efficient search.
  - Generality limits methodological guidance (SRC-BH-019, fetched).
  - It needs measurable, repetitive processes.
- **Validation:** Systematic reviews mostly synthesize case reports (for example 68 healthcare papers of "fair" quality; SRC-BH-018). No controlled effect estimate was located. Status: adoption and case outcomes.
- **Cost/time:** High (trained belts, projects of 3–6 months [GK]).
- **Reproducibility:** High for statistical steps, lower for the Define and Analyze judgments.
- **Adoption:** Very high.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - (1) A **Control/sustain** concept after the next responsible action. EDF stops at sufficiency.
  - (2) **Statistical cause verification** as an optional evidence tier.
  - (3) De Mast and Lokkerbol's critique applies to EDF too. As a generic grammar, EDF gives little guidance on *efficient search* for origins (see KT and structured troubleshooting below).
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Quantitative verification of causal claims and a sustainment phase.

### C3. Theory of Constraints (TOC)

- **Intended problem:** Finding the single constraint that limits system throughput.
- **Mechanism:** The five focusing steps, plus Thinking Processes such as the Current Reality Tree, a logic tree from undesirable effects to core problems with sufficiency logic [GK].
- **Evidence:** Throughput, inventory, and operating-expense data; logic review ("categories of legitimate reservation") [GK].
- **Strengths:**
  - Strong prioritization: one leverage point at a time.
  - The Current Reality Tree is a formal cause-effect tree with explicit sufficiency checks, which EDF's origin network lacks.
- **Limitations:** Single-constraint assumption; logic trees can be built to confirm prior beliefs.
- **Validation:** A meta-analysis of 80+ published applications found operational and financial improvements but **no failure reports**, which points to publication bias. Reports came mainly from manufacturing (SRC-BH-020; SRC-BH-021, fetched: "moderate, but not large" samples). Status: biased case-survey evidence.
- **Cost/time:** Low to moderate.
- **Reproducibility:** Moderate for operational constraints, low for Thinking Processes.
- **Adoption:** Moderate.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - **Leverage concentration**: in control-point ranking, ask whether one point dominates (a constraint) before spreading effort.
  - TOC's legitimate-reservation tests (causality existence, cause sufficiency, additional cause, predicted effect) are a ready-made checklist for EDF origin-network review [GK].
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Prioritization clarity and formal logic checks on causal claims.

### C4. Operational Excellence (OpEx; Shingo-type models)

- **Intended problem:** Sustained excellence in operations through principles, systems, and culture.
- **Mechanism:** Assessment against excellence models or principles.
- **Validation:** A systematic review finds no single underlying theory meeting criteria for a good theory. It also suggests business excellence models may be detrimental to sustaining excellence (SRC-BH-040 and related search results). Status: weak.
- **What EDF could learn:** Little directly. OpEx models are maturity assessments, not causal diagnosis.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Benchmarked maturity scoring.

### C5. Business Process Management (BPM)

- **Intended problem:** Managing and improving end-to-end processes.
- **Mechanism:** Six core elements (strategic alignment, governance, methods, IT, people, culture) derived from maturity-model research (SRC-BH-039). Diagnosis uses process modeling and analysis.
- **Validation:** No empirical performance evidence was located in this search (SRC-BH-039 search). Status: framework and adoption.
- **What EDF could learn:** Process models as an explicit representation for **Propagation** in organizational analyses.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Formal process notation (for example BPMN) and tool support.

### C6. Process Mining

- **Intended problem:** Discovering, checking, and improving real processes from event logs (SRC-BH-035).
- **Mechanism:**
  - Discovery: build a process model from the log.
  - Conformance checking: replay the log against a model to find deviations.
  - Enhancement: for example, find bottlenecks from timestamps (SRC-BH-034, fetched).
- **Evidence:** System event logs.
- **Strengths:**
  - Objective, complete-population evidence on *actual* process behavior.
  - Highly reproducible on the same log.
  - Its guiding principles are directly relevant: "Event data should be treated as first-class citizens" (trustworthy, complete, well-defined semantics); "Log extraction should be driven by questions"; "Models should be treated as purposeful abstractions of reality"; "Process mining should be a continuous process" (SRC-BH-034, fetched).
- **Limitations:**
  - Only sees what systems log.
  - Data finding, merging, and cleaning is a major challenge (Challenge C1).
  - Small logs do not support reliable conclusions (Challenge C2) (SRC-BH-034).
  - Blind to *why*: motives, politics, culture.
- **Validation:** Algorithmic quality (fitness, precision) is well studied [GK]. Organizational diagnostic impact evidence was not located. Status: technically validated for process reconstruction, not for organizational diagnosis.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate to high (data extraction); tooling exists.
- **Reproducibility:** High.
- **Adoption:** Growing rapidly.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - Adopt the event-data **quality criteria** (trustworthy, complete, defined semantics) as part of Evaluate Evidence.
  - Adopt **"question-driven extraction"**, which is EDF's Focus applied to data collection.
  - Adopt **conformance checking** as a concrete method for "formal vs actual".
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Objectivity, completeness, and reproducibility of process evidence.

### C7. Value Stream Mapping

Covered under C1. Specific limitations: static, no variability, manual (SRC-BH-033 and search summaries).

## D. Causal and failure-analysis methods

### D1. Root Cause Analysis, 5 Whys, and Fishbone (Ishikawa)

- **Intended problem:** Finding causes of an adverse event or defect.
- **Mechanism:**
  - 5 Whys: iterative "why" questioning along one chain.
  - Fishbone: brainstormed causes grouped under categories (for example the 6Ms) [GK].
  - Healthcare RCA: a team-based investigation producing causal statements and actions.
- **Evidence:** Interviews, records, team knowledge.
- **Strengths:** Cheap, fast, teachable. Card concedes the pedagogical value of 5 Whys (SRC-BH-025).
- **Limitations:**
  - 5 Whys oversimplifies complex problems to a single chain of causes and obscures how processes fail (SRC-BH-025, fetched).
  - Peerally et al. identify eight problems with healthcare RCA, including the reductionist implication of a single "root" cause, poor analysis quality, and weak feedback (SRC-BH-024).
  - Fishbone treats causes as parallel branches and cannot represent interaction or feedback. No controlled comparison of fishbone against unstructured brainstorming was located in this search.
- **Validation:**
  - RCA appears useful for *identifying* causes but has weak evidence for *preventing recurrence*. Only 2 studies in one review could, to some extent, attribute safety improvement to RCA (SRC-BH-026).
  - VA studies were retrospective and used heterogeneous effectiveness measures (SRC-BH-027).
  - Earlier literature offered anecdotal evidence only (SRC-BH-075).
  - Status: high adoption, weak outcome evidence, no reliability evidence for 5 Whys.
- **Cost/time:** Low (5 Whys: minutes; RCA: weeks).
- **Reproducibility:** Low. The answer depends on who asks.
- **Adoption:** Ubiquitous.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - EDF's origin network already embodies the main critique of single-cause RCA. That is an EDF strength backed by evidence (see also SRC-BH-070: an average of 5.9 contributing factors per diagnostic-error case).
  - From **RCA2** (SRC-BH-028, fetched), EDF can adopt the **Action Hierarchy**. Stronger actions are physical or structural changes such as forcing functions and redesign. Weaker actions are training and policy reminders (SRC-BH-028/029). This gives the Influence and Durability dimension of control-point ranking a grounded ordinal scale.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Speed and familiarity. For a simple, reversible, local issue, 5 Whys is comparable to EDF-0.

### D2. Fault Tree Analysis (FTA)

- **Intended problem:** Deducing combinations of basic events that lead to a defined top event, mainly in engineered systems (SRC-BH-030).
- **Mechanism:** Top-down Boolean logic tree with AND/OR gates. Minimal cut sets show the smallest combinations sufficient for failure, and the tree can be quantified with probabilities (SRC-BH-030 table of contents).
- **Evidence:** Component failure data and system design knowledge.
- **Strengths:**
  - Formal logic separates **jointly necessary** (AND) from **alternative** (OR) causes.
  - Minimal cut sets directly identify control points.
  - Quantifiable.
- **Limitations:**
  - Poor fit for adaptive human and organizational behavior.
  - Assumes a binary, decomposable system.
  - Static.
- **Validation:** Mathematical soundness is established. Accuracy depends on how complete the model is. Status: engineering standard, codified in NRC practice.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate to high.
- **Reproducibility:** High once the tree is agreed; tree construction varies between analysts [GK].
- **Adoption:** High in safety-critical engineering.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - Mark origin-network links as **AND (jointly necessary) or OR (independently sufficient)**. This one notation change would make control-point logic explicit: removing any element of an AND-set prevents the outcome, while OR-branches each need their own control.
  - Cut-set thinking supports control-point ranking.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Logical precision and quantification in engineered subsystems.

### D3. Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (FMEA)

- **Intended problem:** Anticipating failure modes before they occur and prioritizing them.
- **Mechanism:** Enumerate failure modes per process step. Score severity, occurrence, and detectability, and multiply them into a Risk Priority Number (RPN) [GK].
- **Evidence:** Team expertise and process maps.
- **Strengths:** Prospective; helps multidisciplinary teams map and discuss a process (SRC-BH-031, fetched).
- **Limitations:**
  - **Reliability is poor:** two teams analyzing the same process did not reproduce each other's results (SRC-BH-032, secondary summary of Shebl et al. 2009).
  - **Validity is weak:** the teams missed omitted-dose failures, which were the most common failures in actual incident reports. Correlations of severity and probability with incident data were not significant.
  - **The RPN is mathematically invalid** because it multiplies ordinal scales (SRC-BH-031, fetched).
- **Validation:** Negative evidence for healthcare FMEA. The authors recommend not relying solely on FMEA to set priorities (SRC-BH-031).
- **Cost/time:** High; perceived as time-consuming (SRC-BH-031 context).
- **Reproducibility:** Low (demonstrated).
- **Adoption:** High; required by some regulators [GK].
- **What EDF could learn:** This is the **most direct warning for EDF's step 8**. EDF ranks control points on four ordinal dimensions (influence, controllability, cost, confidence). EDF must never combine them arithmetically. It should use dominance or Pareto ordering, or explicit trade-off reasoning. The spec's "no false precision" clause is consistent with this and should be made explicit.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Prospective, process-step-by-step coverage of how things can fail.

### D4. Kepner-Tregoe (KT) Problem Analysis

- **Intended problem:** Finding the cause of a deviation from expected performance.
- **Mechanism:**
  - Specify the problem along what/where/when/extent on **IS / IS NOT** dimensions.
  - Identify **distinctions** of the IS side and **changes** near onset.
  - Generate possible causes from those distinctions and changes.
  - Test each cause against the specification: it must explain both the IS and the IS NOT.
  - Verify (SRC-BH-046; practitioner guides).
- **Evidence:** Precise factual specification of the deviation.
- **Strengths:**
  - **Hypothesis discrimination** using contrast cases.
  - An efficient search strategy, which is exactly the gap de Mast and Lokkerbol identify in generic methods (SRC-BH-019).
- **Limitations:** Best for a single deviation with one dominant cause. Weaker on multicausal, emergent organizational outcomes.
- **Validation:**
  - No independent controlled evaluation of KT was located. A vendor white paper proposes expert rating of the quality of problem analysis but reports no validation (SRC-BH-047).
  - Indirect support: structured troubleshooting, which combines a domain-independent strategy with a multi-level functional model of the system, let technicians solve about **twice as many malfunctions in less time** than traditional training (SRC-BH-048, fetched; this is not KT itself).
  - Status: vendor claims plus indirect experimental support for structured diagnostic strategy.
- **Cost/time:** Low to moderate (hours to days).
- **Reproducibility:** Moderate to high; the specification is explicit.
- **Adoption:** High in IT problem management, manufacturing, and nuclear [GK].
- **What EDF could learn:** This is the **highest-value procedural import**. EDF has no step that uses contrast evidence to discriminate between candidate origins. Two additions would fix that:
  - An **IS / IS NOT / distinctions / changes** sub-step inside Manifestations, or between Origin Network and Evidence.
  - A rule that any proposed origin must explain both where the outcome occurred and where it did not.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Efficient, testable discrimination between candidate causes.

### D5. Systemic accident analysis: AcciMap, STAMP/CAST, HFACS (Rasmussen tradition)

- **Intended problem:** Explaining accidents as products of a whole sociotechnical system, from government and regulators down to frontline work and equipment (SRC-BH-063).
- **Mechanism:**
  - **AcciMap** maps causal factors across hierarchical levels (SRC-BH-063).
  - **STAMP/CAST** models the safety control structure: controllers, control actions, and feedback. It asks why controls were inadequate and rejects single "root cause" thinking (SRC-BH-064).
  - **HFACS** is a hierarchical taxonomy of human and organizational failure categories [GK].
- **Evidence:** Investigation reports, interviews, documents.
- **Strengths:**
  - The closest established analogue to EDF's origin network, propagation, and control points.
  - STAMP's "control structure" maps directly onto EDF control points.
- **Limitations:** Labor-intensive and analyst-dependent.
- **Validation:** This is the **best-validated family found, and the results are sobering**.
  - Salmon et al. had separate analysts apply Accimap, HFACS, and STAMP to one incident. The methods produced significantly different outputs. The authors recommend Accimap plus domain-specific failure taxonomies (SRC-BH-065).
  - Jun et al.: inter-analyst reliability (overlap of causal factors) was 65% for STAMP and 38% for Accimap; validity across methods was 8% (SRC-BH-066).
  - Hulme et al. used signal detection against a referent expert analysis. AcciMap, STAMP-CAST, and AcciNet achieved "comparable and moderate" validity, judged unsatisfactory (SRC-BH-067, fetched).
  - A later reliability study found weak-to-moderate inter-rater correlation, with CAST highest (SRC-BH-068).
  - HFACS taxonomy coding shows low category-level consensus in some settings (SRC-BH-069).
- **Cost/time:** High (weeks).
- **Reproducibility:** Measured, and low to moderate.
- **Adoption:** High in safety science; growing in healthcare and transport.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - (1) **Validation methodology.** EDF can copy this protocol: inter-analyst causal-factor overlap, and signal-detection scoring against a referent analysis. EDF's benchmark harness should report the same metrics so results are comparable with the published 38–65% reliability baselines.
  - (2) **A control-structure representation.** STAMP's higher reliability than Accimap suggests that a more constrained representation (controllers, control actions, feedback) improves agreement between analysts. EDF Control Points could be framed as controllers with control actions and feedback channels.
  - (3) **Hierarchical levels** (Rasmussen) for organizing the origin network.
  - (4) Salmon et al.'s recommendation implies that **domain taxonomies** improve consistency.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Published reliability and validity data, a mature literature, and explicit multi-level structure.

## E. Systems and complexity approaches

### E1. Systems Thinking / System Dynamics

- **Intended problem:** Outcomes produced by feedback structure, delays, and accumulations (stocks).
- **Mechanism:** Causal loop diagrams, stock-and-flow models, and simulation [GK]. The classic organizational diagnosis is the "capability trap" (Repenning and Sterman 2001). Short-term pressure crowds out improvement work, capability erodes, and managers attribute the resulting problems to worker motivation, a self-confirming cycle. Improvement tools fail because of how they interact with the physical, economic, social, and psychological structures they are introduced into (SRC-BH-022, fetched).
- **Evidence:** Time-series data, interviews, model calibration.
- **Strengths:**
  - Explains **policy resistance and worse-before-better dynamics**.
  - Simulation tests interventions before doing them.
  - Formal model tests exist for structure and behavior (SRC-BH-023).
- **Limitations:** Modeling skill is required; quantification can give a false sense of precision; time-consuming.
- **Validation:** There is a well-developed model-testing methodology. Sterman (2000) reportedly argues for dropping the word "validation" in favour of confidence-building tests (SRC-BH-023, secondary). The capability-trap theory is grounded in field studies (SRC-BH-022). Status: strong methodology for building confidence in models; no diagnostic-accuracy statistics.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate (causal loop diagrams) to high (simulation).
- **Reproducibility:** Moderate (diagrams), high (calibrated models).
- **Adoption:** Moderate.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - EDF Propagation is **linear**, from origins to manifestations. It needs **feedback loops, delays, and accumulations**, with notation for reinforcing and balancing loops.
  - Control-point ranking should flag **worse-before-better** and **side effects** of interventions.
  - The capability trap also warns that managers' own attributions are biased evidence. EDF's Evidence step should treat explanations from leaders as data to be tested, not as findings.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Dynamics, feedback, and simulation of interventions.

### E2. Cynefin

- **Intended problem:** Choosing a decision and sense-making mode that fits the nature of the situation (SRC-BH-049, SRC-BH-050).
- **Mechanism:** Classify the situation:
  - Clear: cause and effect are evident.
  - Complicated: cause and effect are discoverable by analysis.
  - Complex: cause and effect are only coherent in retrospect.
  - Chaotic: no perceivable cause and effect.
  - Plus a disorder/confusion state.

  Act accordingly. In the complex domain the mode is probe, sense, respond, using safe-to-fail experiments. Kurtz and Snowden challenge the assumption that cause and effect are always discoverable (SRC-BH-050).
- **Evidence:** Narrative and sense-making workshops [GK].
- **Strengths:** Says *when analysis-first diagnosis is inappropriate*.
- **Limitations:** Critics cite an anecdotal evidence base and limited operational guidance (SRC-BH-050 search context). Domain labels have changed over time.
- **Validation:** No controlled empirical validation located. Status: practitioner adoption and theory.
- **Cost/time:** Low.
- **Reproducibility:** Low; domain assignment is subjective.
- **Adoption:** High in agile and public-sector circles.
- **What EDF could learn:** This matters most for **Diagnostic Sufficiency**. In complex situations, further analysis will not converge, and the "next responsible action" should be a **safe-to-fail probe designed to generate diagnostic evidence**. EDF's sufficiency question already allows this. Making it explicit would stop EDF analyses from over-investigating situations that cannot be analysed to convergence.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Explicit treatment of the limits of causal analysis.

### E3. Organizational Network Analysis (ONA)

- **Intended problem:** Diagnosing informal coordination, bottlenecks, isolation, and over-reliance on central people (SRC-BH-036).
- **Mechanism:** Survey or digital-trace network data analyzed for centrality, brokerage, and density (SRC-BH-036).
- **Evidence:** Relational data, which other methods do not collect.
- **Strengths:**
  - Makes the informal organization visible and measurable.
  - The underlying constructs have meta-analytic support: network position, especially indegree centrality, relates to job performance and career success (SRC-BH-037).
- **Limitations:** Survey burden, privacy, and snapshot timing. Network position does not establish cause.
- **Validation:** Constructs show validity at the individual level (SRC-BH-037). ONA as an organizational diagnostic has case evidence only (SRC-BH-036, a 23-company consortium per search summary). Status: construct-validated, diagnosis-unvalidated.
- **Cost/time:** Moderate.
- **Reproducibility:** High for the same data.
- **Adoption:** Moderate, growing.
- **What EDF could learn:** Add relational evidence as a source type. Organizational propagation paths are often network paths, so ONA can **evidence** EDF's "informational/organizational propagation".
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Quantified evidence on informal structure.

### E4. Enterprise Architecture (EA)

- **Intended problem:** Aligning business, information, application, and technology structures [GK].
- **Mechanism:** Architecture models and gap analysis between current and target states [GK].
- **Validation:** Only about half of the EA articles reviewed provide empirical evidence for their value claims. Outcomes are often attributed to EA without considering alternative explanations. EA enables value rather than creating it directly (SRC-BH-038, fetched). Status: weak.
- **What EDF could learn:** Current-state/target-state gap modeling. Also, the warning about attributing outcomes without ruling out alternatives applies to EDF validation claims.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Formal structural models of IT and business dependencies.

## F. Culture assessments and appreciative approaches

### F1. Culture assessment instruments (OCAI / Competing Values Framework; Denison DOCS)

- **Intended problem:** Diagnosing culture type or effectiveness-related culture traits.
- **Mechanism:**
  - OCAI: respondents distribute 100 points across four culture types (clan, adhocracy, market, hierarchy), for both the current and the preferred culture (SRC-BH-045; search summary).
  - Denison: 60 items across 12 indices (search summary of vendor material).
- **Evidence:** Employee surveys aggregated to organization level.
- **Strengths:** Standardized, normed, scalable, and repeatable.
- **Limitations:**
  - Denison, Nieminen and Kotrba (an author team that includes the vendor) conclude that "the reliability and validity of most instruments in this category is quite limited." Five of nine effectiveness instruments had little or no research after initial publication. An earlier review found no reliability or validity evidence for 10 of 18 culture surveys (SRC-BH-043, fetched).
  - They name three open challenges: confirmatory testing of nested models, rules for aggregating to organization level, and criterion-related validity (SRC-BH-043).
  - For the CVF/OCAI, a meta-analysis of 84 studies found culture types related to effectiveness. It also found mixed support for the framework's nomological validity and no support for parts of its internal structure (SRC-BH-044, fetched).
- **Validation:** The best psychometric evidence in this inventory, and still limited. Status: partial construct validation; no diagnostic-accuracy evidence linking a profile to the right intervention.
- **Cost/time:** Low to moderate.
- **Reproducibility:** High (instrument), moderate (interpretation).
- **Adoption:** Very high.
- **What EDF could learn:** When EDF uses survey evidence about "culture", it must address **aggregation** (whether individual responses legitimately describe the organization) and **criterion validity** (SRC-BH-043). EDF's own example of weak outcome wording, "The launch culture was broken", fits this literature: culture claims are inferences requiring measurement, not observations.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Standardized measurement, norms, and longitudinal comparability.

### F2. Appreciative Inquiry (AI)

- **Intended problem:** Generative change that builds on strengths, through the Discovery, Dream, Design, Destiny cycle [GK].
- **Mechanism:** Positive-question inquiry; it is explicitly *not* problem diagnosis.
- **Evidence:** Stories and participant narratives.
- **Strengths:** Engagement and ownership. Transformational outcomes are associated with new knowledge and generative metaphors (SRC-BH-051).
- **Limitations:** Can suppress negative evidence, and it is not designed to explain failures.
- **Validation:**
  - A healthcare systematic review of 33 studies included one randomized controlled trial (RCT); quality was generally poor; there is "minimal empirical evidence" of effectiveness (SRC-BH-053, fetched).
  - A Norwegian Institute of Public Health (FHI) review found no evidence that AI outperforms other OD methods (SRC-BH-054).
  - In one organization, eight sites ran the same AI process. Four transformed, two changed incrementally, and two showed little change. Positive affect at the summits did *not* predict change; generativity and contextual factors did (SRC-BH-052, fetched).
- **Cost/time:** Moderate (summits).
- **Reproducibility:** Low.
- **Adoption:** Moderate to high in OD.
- **What EDF could learn:**
  - (1) Look for **positive deviance**: contrast cases where the outcome did *not* occur. This is the same logic as KT's IS NOT.
  - (2) The multi-site AI finding is itself an organizational-diagnosis lesson. An identical intervention gave different outcomes depending on context (leadership credibility, pre-existing shared concerns). That supports EDF's insistence on declared System Context.
- **Where it outperforms EDF:** Mobilizing participation and readiness for change.

## G. Clinical and evidence-based organizational diagnosis traditions

The field review is in `lit-org-diagnosis.md`. In summary:

- **Levinson (1972)** uses a clinical, case-study diagnosis with extensive history-taking (SRC-BH-057).
- **Harrison (2005)** and **Harrison and Shirom (1999)** cover models, methods, and client processes; "sharp-image" diagnosis that focuses on a few critical, actionable issues; and **multiframe diagnosis** (SRC-BH-055, SRC-BH-056).
- **McFillen et al. (2013)** propose evidence-based diagnosis modelled on medicine. They argue that a lack of rigour and the resulting misdiagnoses likely contribute to change failure, which is an untested claim (SRC-BH-059).

What EDF could learn: multiframe diagnosis (EDF Diagnostic Calibration lenses are a version of this) and the "sharp image" criterion (focus on a few feasible, high-impact issues). Both match EDF's Focus and Sufficiency.

## Where competitors outperform EDF

These are areas where an established method is likely better than EDF v0.3 today, given current evidence and design. Each is a candidate improvement or a boundary on EDF's claims.

1. **Published reliability and validity data (STAMP-CAST, AcciMap, HFACS; to a lesser degree culture instruments).** EDF has none. Until EDF reports inter-analyst agreement and criterion validity with the same metrics (SRC-BH-066, SRC-BH-067), it cannot claim parity, let alone superiority. CAST's 65% reliability on one accident (SRC-BH-066) is the nearest published bar.
2. **Efficient discrimination among candidate causes (Kepner-Tregoe; structured troubleshooting).** EDF lists origins and evaluates evidence but has no explicit contrast-testing step. Generic methods are documented to be weak at efficient diagnosis (SRC-BH-019). A structured strategy plus a domain model doubled troubleshooting success in an experiment (SRC-BH-048).
3. **Objective, reproducible process evidence (Process Mining, Lean/VSM, Six Sigma).** For process-driven outcomes, these produce population-level, replayable evidence. EDF's evidence step has no defined tiers or quality criteria.
4. **Statistical verification of causal claims (Six Sigma).** EDF rates "confidence" qualitatively. DMAIC can test a cause empirically.
5. **Logical precision about joint and alternative causation (Fault Tree Analysis; TOC logic trees).** EDF's origin network does not say whether origins are jointly necessary or independently sufficient. That ambiguity affects which control points actually prevent recurrence.
6. **Dynamics and feedback (System Dynamics).** EDF propagation is effectively linear. Organizational outcomes are often generated by reinforcing loops and delays, as in the capability trap (SRC-BH-022). EDF currently has no way to express worse-before-better or policy resistance.
7. **Organizational content coverage (Burke-Litwin, Nadler-Tushman, Weisbord, 7S, Star).** For organizational use, EDF offers no checklist of organizational components, so origin coverage depends heavily on the analyst. Salmon et al.'s recommendation to combine systemic maps with domain taxonomies (SRC-BH-065) suggests that content prompts improve consistency. The taxonomies themselves are weakly validated, though.
8. **Grounded action-strength ranking (RCA2 Action Hierarchy).** EDF's four ranking criteria have no anchored scales. The Action Hierarchy gives an ordinal scale for intervention durability (SRC-BH-028).
9. **Ongoing monitoring and sustainment (Balanced Scorecard; DMAIC Control).** EDF ends at "next responsible action". Competitors build in control plans and leading indicators that would let EDF's Validate verb operate.
10. **Recognizing the limits of analysis (Cynefin).** In complex domains, probe-sense-respond is likely more appropriate than extended causal analysis. EDF's sufficiency step should say so explicitly.
11. **Standardized organization-wide measurement (OCAI, Denison, Burke-Litwin surveys, ONA).** For culture, climate, and informal networks, survey and network instruments give scalable, comparable evidence that EDF's narrative evidence step cannot.
12. **Engagement and readiness for change (Appreciative Inquiry; dialogic OD).** Where the next responsible action depends on collective ownership, dialogic methods may outperform expert diagnosis. The evidence for AI is weak too, so this is plausible rather than demonstrated.

### Where EDF plausibly has an edge (stated with equal caution)

These are plausible strengths, not demonstrated ones:

- **A multi-causal origin network by default.** This matches the evidence that real failures are multifactorial (SRC-BH-070: 5.9 factors per case) and avoids the single-cause critiques of 5 Whys and RCA (SRC-BH-024, SRC-BH-025).
- **Separating origins from control points.** Few organizational models do this; STAMP-CAST does it implicitly.
- **An explicit Diagnostic Sufficiency stopping rule tied to the next action.** No content model above has a stopping rule.
- **Scalability across EDF-0, EDF-1, and EDF-2.** Lightweight relative to Burke-Litwin surveys or CAST.

None of these has been tested empirically.
