Method: apply the following diagnostic method (experimental EDF variant) internally, then answer the scored items.

1. State the problem. Restate the question and the outcome in factual terms.

2. Review the evidence. Note what each evidence item shows and separate observation from inference.

4. Build the origin network. Allow several interacting causes, feedback loops, delays, and causes that sit outside the unit that shows the symptom. Separate the mechanism that creates the problem from controls that merely reveal or contain it.

5. Grade every causal link. Causal only when the evidence shows temporal order, a mechanism, covariation, and the main rival ruled out, or an intervention or natural experiment shows the effect. Co-occurrence without that is association only. A contrast case where the supposed cause is present without the effect, or the effect occurs without the cause, counts against the link.

6. Judge actions by what they do under every live hypothesis. Harmful: likely to cause damage even if the leading hypothesis is true, such as punishing messengers or the wrong party, suppressing information, rewarding a gamed metric, disabling a protective control, or undermining a deliberate strategy. Premature: sensible only if a still-unconfirmed hypothesis is true. Preferred: changes an established mechanism, or cheaply and reversibly resolves the uncertainty that most affects the decision.

7. Decide sufficiency. Evidence is sufficient when it supports the next substantive decision, including the decision that no intervention is warranted because the presenting problem does not exist or is an accepted tradeoff. It is insufficient when the best action depends on which of several live hypotheses is true.

8. Choose next evidence by its power to discriminate between live hypotheses or to change the decision, not by how much it would confirm the current favorite.
