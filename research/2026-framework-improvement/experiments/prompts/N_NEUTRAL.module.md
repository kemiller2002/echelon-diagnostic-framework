Method: use the following disciplined review protocol.

Step 1 - Restate the question.
Write down, in plain factual terms, what is being asked and what result is being explained. Separate the result itself from anyone's interpretation of it. Note what a good answer would let someone do next.

Step 2 - Inventory the information.
List each piece of information and what it directly shows. Distinguish what was directly observed or recorded from what someone concluded or believes. Note who or what produced each item, when, and how it could be mistaken or incomplete. Note gaps: important things that are not described at all.

Step 3 - Generate explanations.
Write several distinct explanations for the result, including less obvious ones. Do not stop at the first explanation that fits. Explanations may combine several factors; a result can have more than one contributor.

Step 4 - Weigh each explanation.
For each explanation, list the information that fits it, the information that does not fit it, and the information that would be expected if it were true but is absent. Judge each explanation on its own merits rather than by how plausible it sounds. Consider whether two things that appear together might both follow from something else, or whether the order of events rules a link in or out.

Step 5 - Trace connections.
For each claimed link between conditions, decide whether the information shows the link, merely shows the two appearing together, counts against the link, or says nothing either way. Check the timing and the mechanism. Keep direction straight.

Step 6 - Check consistency.
Make sure the judgements in steps 4 and 5 agree with each other. If one explanation is favoured, the links it depends on must also be supported. Look for any piece of information you have ignored and account for it.

Step 7 - Consider actions.
For each possible action, ask what it would change, which explanation it depends on, how confident that explanation is, what it would cost, what side effects or new problems it could create, and whether it can be undone. Favour actions that address what actually produces the result over actions that only change how the result looks. Favour actions whose benefit does not depend on an unconfirmed explanation, and steps that would resolve the main remaining doubt cheaply.

Step 8 - Decide how much is known.
Ask whether what is known is enough to justify a substantive commitment now, or whether the main doubt should be resolved first. State what remains uncertain and how much it matters to the choice.

Step 9 - Choose next information.
Identify which additional information would most change the conclusion or the choice of action, and which would merely confirm what is already clear.

Step 10 - Review.
Re-read the original question and your answers. Remove any claim not grounded in the information provided. Check that confidence matches the strength of the information rather than the fluency of the story.

Apply this protocol internally, then answer the scored items.
