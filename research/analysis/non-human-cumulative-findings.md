# Non-Human Cumulative Findings and Theory Impact

## Level 1 — direct observations

- Twelve completed experiment-like records were located.
- None preserves raw output data, exact prompts, model identity, commit, or failed-run ledger.
- Three R1 records publish exact field-agreement percentages.
- Five R1 records summarize five analytical lenses.
- One ablation compares old and refined wording on one POS case.
- Three method comparisons share the same Chernobyl framing and narrative evaluator.
- IFVS-001 and LRC-001 have zero completed runs.

## Level 2 — experiment-specific conclusions

- ABL-002: revised Propagation wording is clearer in the authored POS example.
- Apollo 13: static EDF output has difficulty expressing repeated state changes.
- Boeing: a large origin network can be organized into descriptive layers.
- Apollo 11/Pixar/Toyota: EDF fields can describe selected success narratives.
- CFCV family: EDF, RCA, FMEA, and FTA have definitionally different scopes.

## Level 3 — cross-experiment patterns

- Repository-authored outputs repeatedly conform to EDF's structural fields.
- Selected complex cases favor multi-origin narratives.
- Success cases repeatedly emphasize feedback, early problem visibility, and learning.
- The research program consistently preserves conceptual caveats better than it preserves execution evidence.

## Level 4 — possible mechanisms

- Explicit field prompts may reduce omission and causal-chain compression.
- Whole-system lens instructions may align scope across generated analyses.
- Structure/behavior wording may cue distinct representations.
- Shared evaluator and prompt structure may account for much of reported agreement.

These mechanisms are competing explanations; none is isolated.

## Level 5 — transferable principles

### Principle 1: Preserve execution before synthesis

Support: every experiment loses raw evidence. Contradiction: none. Boundary: applies to computational experiments and generated artifacts. Confidence: strongly supported as a research-system requirement. Consequence: require immutable run bundles.

### Principle 2: Structural conformity is not reproducibility

Support: R1 reports and absent independent runs. Boundary: current corpus. Confidence: strongly supported. Consequence: rename current scores and use independent reruns with executable metrics.

### Principle 3: Architecture is not demonstrated capability

Support: LRC-001/IFVS-001 planned status versus comparative claims. Confidence: strongly supported. Consequence: keep evidence evolution and independent reproducibility labeled unresolved.

### Principle 4: Diverse cases do not replace diverse execution systems

Support: case breadth paired with shared method/evaluator. Confidence: strongly supported. Consequence: vary models, prompts, seeds, and evaluators independently.

## Level 7 — engineering implications

- Store a machine-readable run manifest for every attempt, including failures.
- Hash prompts, evidence packages, configs, and evaluator rubrics.
- Separate formatting agreement, semantic overlap, factual correctness, and intervention utility.
- Use matched structured-template and natural-analysis controls.
- Blind evaluators to treatment and randomize output order.
- Treat model/provider/version as experimental variables.
- Instrument tokens, latency, retries, invalid outputs, and evaluator cost.

## What would falsify the preferred synthesis

The “coherent but unvalidated” conclusion would be falsified by existing, discoverable raw run bundles demonstrating independent executions, controlled baselines, and reproducible scoring. None was located. Future high-quality experiments can supersede this assessment.
