# Chernobyl Longitudinal Evidence-Sensitivity Study

## Research opportunity

Execute LRC-001 across EP-013A, EP-013B, and EP-013C to test whether diagnoses update proportionally as evidence changes.

## Background and unknowns

Evidence evolution is described as a differentiator, but all related claims are planned. It is unknown which fields remain stable, which change, and whether confidence appropriately tracks evidence.

## Origin documents and evidence

- `docs/edf/validation/longitudinal/LRC-001-chernobyl.md`, full experimental design.
- `docs/framework-engineering/longitudinal-reference-cases.md`, “Diagnostic Stability” and “Experimental Design.”
- `docs/edf/validation/evidence-packages/EP-013A-chernobyl-1986.md`, limitation and exclusions.
- EP-013B, EP-013C, FCR-0100 through FCR-0102, and RQS-001.

Specific assumption: frozen evidence packages isolate evidence state sufficiently to prevent hindsight contamination.

## Dependencies

Independent source audit of each evidence package; frozen scoring rubric; separate analysts or washout/counterbalancing to prevent cross-run memory.

## Suggested REP and methodology

REP: `REP-EDF-DIAGNOSTIC-STABILITY-001`.

Blind analysts to later packages. Compare field-level persistence, additions, removals, confidence movement, and control-point rank changes. Predefine proportionality rules before opening later packages.

## Expected outputs and success criteria

- Three immutable run sets and a longitudinal comparison report.
- Success: stable findings persist, material new evidence produces localized justified changes, and no later evidence leaks into earlier runs.

Recommended agent: longitudinal validation lead with Chernobyl subject-matter reviewer. Estimated effort: 6–10 weeks. Expected knowledge gain: very high.
