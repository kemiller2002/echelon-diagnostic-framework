## Current Conclusion

No completed cross-executor reproducibility result exists in the repository.

The repository also did not preserve enough historical execution detail to demonstrate computational execution reconstructability for the R1 results.

Earlier R1 case summaries reported "High" or "Very High reproducibility." Under the current Claim and Confidence Policy, those labels are reclassified as **historical internal structural agreement** because:

- the analyses were produced inside a shared research process;
- named analytical lenses were not independent execution systems;
- exact prompts and model identities were not preserved;
- raw lens outputs were not preserved;
- scoring denominators and executable calculations were not preserved;
- failed and invalid runs were not recorded;
- no independent human or controlled multi-model replication was completed.

The historical case files are preserved unchanged. This document records the current interpretation.

## Operational Definitions

EDF uses qualified terms because scientific disciplines use "reproducibility" differently.

- **Execution reconstructability:** the same declared inputs, versions, code, and scoring procedure can be rerun from preserved artifacts.
- **Within-condition repeatability:** repeated runs under explicitly same conditions meet preregistered agreement criteria.
- **Cross-executor reproducibility:** materially different executors, such as separate analysts or model families, meet preregistered semantic and factual-support criteria while using the same frozen task/evidence.
- **Cross-case replication/generalization:** the measured effect persists on held-out cases relevant to the same research question.

Historical R1 results currently establish none of these as a completed performance result. They document internal structural agreement in a shared research process.

Multiple analytical lenses generated by the same model or research process do not count as independent executors.

## Required Metrics

Future studies must separate:

- structural validity;
- semantic origin overlap;
- semantic propagation agreement;
- evidence-supported claim precision/recall;
- unsupported-claim rate;
- control-point overlap and utility;
- evaluator agreement;
- run failure rate;
- cost and latency.

No aggregate "reproducibility" label should hide these dimensions.

## What Would Raise Confidence

Confidence in independent reproducibility can increase only after a completed study preserves:

- frozen FCR;
- exact prompts and evidence hashes;
- model/participant identities at the appropriate privacy level;
- raw outputs;
- evaluator outputs;
- executable scoring;
- failed/invalid runs;
- preregistered thresholds;
- independent replication source.
