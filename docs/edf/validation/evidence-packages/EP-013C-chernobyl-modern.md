# EP-013C - Chernobyl Modern Evidence State

Status: Planned evidence package.

## Purpose

Represents modern historical understanding of Chernobyl as a complex sociotechnical accident.

## Evidence-State Framing

Modern synthesis generally treats Chernobyl as the product of interacting reactor design flaws, operational decisions, safety culture failures, regulatory weakness, training gaps, information suppression, and political/organizational context.

## Source Notes

- Later summaries describe Chernobyl as involving flawed reactor design, inadequately trained personnel, and lack of safety culture.
- OECD/NEA summaries describe the accident as involving lack of safety culture, reactor design deficiencies, and operator actions / procedure deviations.

## Evidence Categories

Technical:

- RBMK design vulnerabilities.
- Positive void coefficient.
- Control rod design / positive scram effect.
- Reactor instability.
- Inadequate containment and safety design.

Operational:

- Turbine rundown test conditions.
- Operator actions.
- Procedure deviations.
- Operational context and constraints.

Organizational:

- Safety culture.
- Training.
- Procedure design.
- Risk communication.
- Organizational learning failures.

Regulatory / Governmental:

- Weak independent oversight.
- Soviet nuclear governance.
- Information suppression.
- Production and political pressures.

Information:

- Evolution of the historical explanation.
- Differences between early operator-error framing and later sociotechnical understanding.
- Evidence that later analyses shifted responsibility toward design and systemic factors.
