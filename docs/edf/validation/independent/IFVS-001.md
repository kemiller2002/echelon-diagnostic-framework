# IFVS-001 - Independent Framework Validation Study

Status: Working draft

IFVS = Independent Framework Validation Study.

IDAR = Independent Diagnostic Analyst Report.

DAR = Diagnostic Agreement Report.

## Purpose

Evaluate whether independent analysts, using only EDF documentation and the supplied Evidence Package, can produce comparable diagnostic analyses of the same problem.

## Terminology

Independent Diagnostic Analysts (IDAs)

Definition:
An Independent Diagnostic Analyst is an individual who independently applies EDF to an Evidence Package without collaboration or prior knowledge of other analysts' work.

Purpose:
The objective is to evaluate framework reproducibility rather than individual correctness.

Agreement is evaluated structurally rather than linguistically. Independent analysts are expected to produce different wording while arriving at comparable diagnostic structures.

## Study Chain

```text
Evidence Package
-> Independent Diagnostic Analyst Reports (IDARs)
-> Diagnostic Agreement Report (DAR)
-> Engineering Change Requests if warranted
-> Updated EDF Specification if justified
```

## Independent Diagnostic Analyst Recruitment

- Operations Manager
- Safety Professional
- Project Manager
- Graduate Student with limited domain expertise

### Business Executive / Operations Leader

Background:
Responsible for organizational decision-making, resource allocation, customer impact, business continuity, and operational performance.

Purpose:
Evaluate whether EDF functions as a common diagnostic language outside technical disciplines.

Expected contribution:

- Organizational perspective
- Business impact
- Strategic control points
- Cross-functional thinking
- Customer and operational consequences

The objective is not technical correctness.

The objective is determining whether EDF enables non-technical professionals to produce a recognizable diagnostic structure.

## Independent Diagnostic Analyst Reflection

Complete immediately after submitting the analysis.

Questions:

1. Which EDF concept contributed the most to your reasoning?
2. Which EDF concept was least intuitive?
3. At what point did EDF change how you were thinking about the problem?
4. Which section required the greatest effort?
5. Which section seemed least valuable?
6. Did EDF encourage you to consider factors you would otherwise have overlooked?
7. Did EDF help you represent uncertainty more clearly?
8. Approximately how long did the analysis require?
9. Would you voluntarily use EDF again?
10. If you could simplify EDF, what would you remove?
11. If you could strengthen EDF, what would you add?
