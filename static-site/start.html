Your first diagnosis

Bring one real outcome.

Choose something current, bounded, and safe to examine: a recurring support escalation, a delayed handoff, an adoption drop, or a process that works unusually well. Your goal is not a perfect answer. It is a more responsible next move.

Use the worksheet

Before you begin

Use facts you can inspect.

  • Good first case

    Local, recent, observable, and connected to an action you can test.

    • Wait or escalate

    High-consequence safety, legal, clinical, or personnel decisions need appropriate experts and governance.

  • Timebox

    Give an EDF–0 pass 15 minutes. Expand only if the uncertainty earns more effort.

EDF–0 worksheet

Write the diagnosis in six passes.

Use short, concrete sentences. If you discover that an earlier field was wrong, revise it. That is the framework working.

  1. 01

    System context

    What system are you primarily analyzing? What larger system materially shapes it? What narrow aspect is in focus?

    “Mobile checkout, within our direct-to-consumer storefront, focused on payment completion after Tuesday’s release.”

    Avoid Naming the whole company or a boundary so narrow that it excludes obvious influences.

  2. 02

    Outcome and manifestations

    What happened? What observable signals show it? Include a time, count, rate, comparison, or direct observation when possible.

    “Payment completion fell from 71% to 54%. Failures are concentrated on mobile app version 8.4.”

    Avoid “The new SDK broke checkout.” That is already an explanation.

  3. 03

    Origin network and propagation

    List at least two plausible contributors. For each, trace how its effect could reach the outcome.

    “SDK timeout → automatic retry → duplicate request lock → failed payment. Competing read: issuer outage → authorization failure.”

    Ask What else could produce the same manifestation?

  4. 04

    Evidence, contradiction, and confidence

    What supports each path? What contradicts it? What source produced the evidence? What remains unknown?

    “Logs show retry locks only on 8.4. Issuer failures are stable. Medium–high qualitative confidence; device-specific behavior remains unknown.”

    Ask What single observation would most change your mind?

  5. 05

    Control points and ranking

    List candidate interventions. Compare them heuristically by influence, controllability, cost, reversibility, speed, and confidence. EDF does not yet have validated numerical weights for this ranking.

    “1. Disable automatic retry. 2. Roll back SDK. 3. Change lock behavior. Retry disable ranks first: fast, reversible, and specific to the modeled path.”

    Avoid Automatically choosing the earliest cause or the most visible symptom.

  6. 06

    Sufficiency and next responsible action

    Do you know enough to act, investigate, or reframe? State what will happen next and what result would support the model.

    “Sufficient to disable retry for 10% of traffic. If lock errors and abandonment fall together, expand; otherwise restore and investigate.”

    Finish with An owner, a bounded action, and a verification signal.

The handoff

Diagnosis is not the decision.

EDF clarifies reality and identifies leverage. Your decision process still has to evaluate options, tradeoffs, ethics, resources, and risk. After execution, return to EDF and ask whether the system changed as expected.
